# Story craft

## What makes an idea work in 60 seconds

Score each candidate on five questions; pitch only ones that pass at least four.

1. **Can the premise be said in one breath and sound impossible?** "A town was buried by a wave of molasses."
2. **Is there a hard detail?** A year, an amount, a distance, a name. Details buy belief.
3. **Is there one person to follow?** A crowd is not a protagonist.
4. **Does it turn?** The ending should not be guessable from the opening line.
5. **Zero homework?** A ten-year-old and a grandparent both get it instantly.

Skip stories that need a history lesson first, that are only tragic with no turn, or that are about living
private people. Prefer stories with several independent reliable sources.

Hook lines in the idea list should open a question the viewer has to close. Shapes that work (write your own):
- the impossible claim: "He sold a famous landmark he never owned."
- the absurd number: "One mistake cost a company $440 million in 45 minutes."
- the reversal: "The man who invented it spent his life trying to ban it."

## Script shape

Length budget: 45-60 s ≈ 115-150 English words (≈ 95-125 Hebrew words); 30-40 s ≈ 75-100 English words.

| Beat | Lines | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook (cold open) | 1 | The impossible claim, flat and plain, 9 words or fewer, over a picture that already shows it. No greeting, no "did you know", no "in this video". |
| Stakes | 1 | Make leaving feel like a loss: "And it only took him one letter." |
| Setup | 1-2 | Who, where, when, in the fewest words. |
| Escalation | 4-6 | Every line is a consequence of the last. Cut anything that is merely interesting. |
| Turn | 1 | The surprise, about 70-80% of the way through. |
| Landing | 1 | Pays off the hook and flows back into line 1, so the loop replays seamlessly. |

## Line rules

- One idea per line, under ~14 words; each line is one scene. Lines over ~9 words get a cut in the storyboard.
- Spoken rhythm: short words, contractions, active verbs.
- Concrete nouns the animation can draw: letters, gold, a ship, a crown, a newspaper.
- Say numbers the easy way: "twice", "half the city", not "on two separate occasions".
- No calls to action inside the story; "follow for part 2" belongs in the caption.
- Hebrew: everyday spoken Hebrew, not literary; write numbers as digits (the captions handle RTL).

## Upload text

- **Title:** the hook in under 60 characters, curiosity first.
- **Description:** one or two sentences that tease the turn without giving it away, plus "Based on the
  documented story of ...".
- **Hashtags:** `#shorts` plus two topic tags.
